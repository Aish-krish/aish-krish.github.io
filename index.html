About

I got into software engineering because I wanted to understand how things worked underneath. That curiosity has mostly stayed intact — it has just gotten more specific over time. These days I am most interested in systems that are hard to reason about: distributed architectures, agents that have to make decisions mid-flight, and the deceptively tricky question of what "reliable" even means when the world is messy.

At Citi, I own backend services that handle real money for real people. That context has been a good teacher. It has made me take correctness seriously and given me a healthy skepticism toward anything that looks like it might fail quietly. I have also spent a lot of time thinking about how AI can make engineers faster — not by replacing judgment, but by automating the parts that do not need it.

I have shipped two production AI tools at work: one that automates API testing using LLMs, and an MCP-based agentic system for orchestrating workflows across our platform. Both started as experiments. Both grew larger than expected. I have learned that is usually a sign you are solving a real problem.

Outside engineering, I run, swim, sing (and yes, I post covers online), watch Formula 1, and rewatch Modern Family when my brain needs a break. I was also once a school magazine sub-editor, which still surprises people more than it should.

I completed two undergraduate degrees simultaneously — at SSN College of Engineering and IIT Madras — which seemed like a reasonable idea at the time and, honestly, mostly was.
