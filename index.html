About

I got into software engineering because I wanted to understand how things worked underneath. That curiosity has mostly stayed intact — it's just gotten more specific over time. These days I'm most interested in systems that are hard to reason about: distributed architectures, agents that have to make decisions mid-flight, and the deceptively tricky question of what "reliable" even means when the world is messy.

At Citi, I own backend services that handle real money for real people. That context has been a good teacher. It's made me take correctness seriously and given me a healthy skepticism toward anything that looks like it might fail quietly. I've also spent a lot of time exploring how AI can make engineers faster — not by replacing judgment, but by automating the parts that don't need it.

I've built two production AI tools at work: one that automates API testing using LLMs, and an MCP-based agentic system for orchestrating workflows across our platform. Both started as experiments. Both grew larger than expected. I've learned that's usually a sign you're solving a real problem.

Outside engineering, I run, swim, sing (yes, I post covers), watch Formula 1, and rewatch Modern Family when my brain needs a break. I was also once a school magazine sub-editor, which still surprises people.

I completed two undergraduate degrees simultaneously — at SSN College of Engineering and IIT Madras — which seemed like a reasonable idea at the time and, honestly, mostly was.

Research

Publications

01
Springer · Circuits, Systems & Signal Processing · Apr 2024
Real-time Tamil Dialect Speech Recognition and Summarization

Built a large-scale dataset of Tamil dialect variation — regional, colloquial, cultural — and used it to fine-tune OpenAI Whisper and NVIDIA NeMo. The goal was making ASR work for how Tamil is actually spoken, not just how it appears in textbooks.

02
ILSUM FIRE 2022 · CEUR-WS · May 2023
Summarising Indian Languages using Machine Learning

Compared mT5-m2m-CrossSum, BERT, XL-Sum, and mT5-small on Hindi, Gujarati, and English summarisation tasks as part of FIRE 2022. A systematic look at how multilingual models transfer — or don't — across structurally different languages.
